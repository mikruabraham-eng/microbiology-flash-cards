Practical laboratory guide

Choose a sterilization method based on the material, intended use, and a validated procedure. The examples below are educational; actual cycles must follow laboratory SOPs and equipment instructions.

MethodPrincipleTypical application
Steam under pressureMoist heat denatures essential proteins.Compatible culture media, instruments, and appropriate laboratory waste.
Dry heatOxidation and heat-induced cellular damage.Suitable glassware, metal, and certain oils or powders.
Membrane filtrationPhysical removal of microorganisms from a liquid.Some heat-sensitive solutions; a 0.22 µm membrane is commonly used for bacterial removal, but does not reliably remove all viruses or endotoxins.
Ionizing radiationDNA and cellular damage.Compatible disposable medical products processed in validated facilities.
UV-C radiationNucleic-acid damage where radiation reaches.Exposed surfaces and air in suitable systems; shadows and limited penetration reduce effectiveness.
Chemical sterilizationValidated chemical inactivation.Compatible heat-sensitive equipment using systems such as ethylene oxide or vaporized hydrogen peroxide.

Basic autoclave practice

  1. Check material compatibility and use the correct container. Do not tightly seal containers unless the system is specifically designed for it.
  2. Avoid overloading; arrange the load to allow steam circulation.
  3. Select the validated cycle for the load. A commonly taught cycle is 121°C at approximately 15 psi gauge pressure, but the required exposure time depends on the load and validated procedure.
  4. Review physical records and appropriate chemical indicators; biological indicators are used according to the laboratory's quality-assurance plan.
  5. After the cycle, confirm pressure has returned to a safe level. Open cautiously and allow hot liquids to cool before handling.
  6. Document cycle parameters, indicator results, failures, and corrective actions.
Remember: A chemical indicator changing color confirms exposure to specified conditions, not sterility. A biological indicator uses resistant spores to evaluate process effectiveness. Sterilization requires a validated process and appropriate monitoring.
